The Weeknight Dinner Guide

Five dinners a week does not require five recipes. It requires a few repeatable shapes, a short shopping list and half an hour of preparation on a Sunday.

Cook shapes, not recipes

A recipe tells you exactly what to buy and exactly what to do. A shape tells you only the structure: something on rice, a traybake, a pasta, an egg dish. You fill the shape with whatever is in the fridge. Shapes survive a missing ingredient; recipes do not.

ShapeTimeHow it goes
Stir-fry on rice20 minRice on first. Fry protein, then vegetables, then a sauce of soy, garlic and ginger.
Traybake40 minProtein and chopped vegetables on one tray with oil and seasoning, in a hot oven.
Pasta20 minStart the sauce when the water goes on. Finish the pasta in the sauce.
Frittata25 minSoften vegetables in an ovenproof pan, pour over beaten eggs, finish under the grill.
One-pot stew45 minBrown, add liquid and pulses or potatoes, simmer with the lid on.

A shopping list that covers the week

Buy by category and you can cook any shape on any night.

  • Three proteins: for example chicken thighs, a tin of chickpeas and a box of eggs.
  • Six vegetables: two that keep (onions, carrots), two for roasting (peppers, courgettes), two green (broccoli, spinach).
  • Three starches: rice, pasta and potatoes.
  • Flavour: garlic, lemons, tinned tomatoes, stock, soy sauce, one fresh herb.

Thirty minutes on Sunday

A little preparation at the weekend removes the slowest steps from every weeknight.

  1. Cook a large pan of rice or grains. Cool it quickly, refrigerate within the hour and use within a day or two, reheating until steaming hot.
  2. Wash and chop the sturdy vegetables and store them in boxes.
  3. Make one sauce or dressing: a tomato sauce, a yogurt and herb dressing, or a simple vinaigrette.
  4. Take anything frozen that you will need on Monday and Tuesday and move it to the fridge.

Cook once, eat twice

Whenever the oven is on, fill it. A second tray of roast vegetables becomes tomorrow’s pasta sauce or frittata filling. Extra roast chicken becomes Monday’s stir-fry.

The order of operations

On the night, start with whatever takes longest and needs least attention: put the oven on, get the water boiling, start the rice. Chop while those heat. Cook the quick things last. Most weeknight dinners that feel frantic have simply been cooked in the wrong order.

When there is nothing in the house

Keep a short list of cupboard dinners for the nights the plan falls apart: pasta with garlic, oil and tinned tomatoes; eggs on toast with frozen spinach; beans on rice with whatever cheese is left. They take fifteen minutes and are better than a late takeaway. The Weeknight Plate Builder suggests a plate from what you have.
